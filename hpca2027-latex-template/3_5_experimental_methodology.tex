\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

FourShadow is implemented in Vortex~\cite{vortex}, the open-source RISC-V GPU, and its performance is measured there: in processor cycles on SimX, the Vortex cycle-level simulator, and end to end on an FPGA. Warp-level MMA with 2:4 sparsity is also measured on a commercial GPU, an NVIDIA RTX 5080, and the two sets of measurements are compared, for the reasons given in Section~\ref{sec:motivation}.

\textbf{Comparison with the RTX 5080.} The feature compared is warp-level MMA with 2:4 structured sparsity. On FourShadow, the dense kernel \texttt{sgemm\_tcu} and the sparse kernel \texttt{sgemm\_tcu\_sp} run on SimX with one core and 8 or 32 threads per warp in the issue width 1, number of warps 4 case (IW-1, NW-4), where the warps are counted per core; the cycles are simulated processor cycles over the in-kernel region. We compare at (IW-1, NW-4) because the FP16 speedups of the two platforms, over the 17 shapes measured on both and in-kernel region against \texttt{clock64} region, differ there by a median of 9.0\% at 8 threads and 6.2\% at 32 threads; the median is 20.5\% and 17.0\% at (IW-2, NW-4) and 27.8\% and 17.0\% at (IW-4, NW-4). On the RTX 5080, under CUDA~13.0, one CTA of four warps executes the PTX instructions \texttt{mma} (dense) and \texttt{mma.sp} (sparse), and the cycles are read with \texttt{clock64} around eight repetitions of the GEMM. The TF32 sparse fixture of the RTX 5080 uses the 1:2 pattern. Sections~\ref{sec:eval_sparse_halves} and~\ref{sec:eval_sparse_meta} report the results.
