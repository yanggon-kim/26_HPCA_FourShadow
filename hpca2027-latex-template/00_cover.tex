\noindent We thank the reviewers. The revision gave priority to the reviewers' \emph{Questions for
revision/rebuttal}: items 1--9 answer them one by one. Beyond these, we also addressed the
\emph{Comments for authors} (item 10). [A]--[F] name the reviews; every reference links into the
revised paper.

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\emph{Addressing questions for revision/rebuttal.}}

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{1. Five boxed findings answer the two questions.} [A][F] FourShadow is the
white-box counterpart of a commercial tensor core. Section~\ref{sec:intro} poses Q1, the
microarchitectural behavior behind NVIDIA's tensor-core features, and Q2, how a tensor core
behaves as its parameters change. The answer boxes:
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 Q2 \#1 (\ref{sec:eval_threads}): per-lane warp-group MMA
throughput peaks at 16 threads per warp.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 Q2 \#2 (\ref{sec:eval_iw}): the front end
limits a wider warpgroup; an issue width of 8 speeds up a $K$ step by 3.6--3.9$\times$, not 8$\times$.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 Q1 \#1 (\ref{sec:eval_delay_a}): in SS mode every
warp-group MMA waits for $A$, on the H100 too.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 Q1 \#2 (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_halves}): 2:4 sparsity
halves only part of the per-$K$ cost.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 Q1 \#3 (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_wg}): that part shrinks as
the warp-group tile widens, on the H100 too.\par

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{2. Every result has one stated baseline.} [B][D][E] The FPGA end-to-end
speedups are over the dense base TCU, alike in the abstract, Section~\ref{sec:intro} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:e2e_fpga}: WGMMA gives 10.18$\times$ and 7.76$\times$ for Llama-3.2-1B prefill and
decode, and 12.39$\times$ and 8.57$\times$ with 2:4 sparsity. The SimX area--performance study
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}) is over the SIMT baseline, as its caption states. Table~\ref{tab:config} states each platform's configuration and enabled features,
and each Evaluation part names its baseline (\ref{sec:method_sw}).

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{3. End-to-end workloads are specified and their gains separated.} [C][D]
Table~\ref{tab:fpga-config} gives the models and input sizes of all seven workloads, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:e2e_fpga} separates WGMMA, sparsity and both over the base TCU: WGMMA alone gives 1.05--10.28$\times$, sparsity alone 1.02--1.51$\times$,
and both 1.76--12.74$\times$.

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{4. FourShadow is measured beside two NVIDIA GPUs.} [B][C]
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} adds an RTX 5080 and an H100. Over 17 shapes, FourShadow's FP16
sparse speedup differs from the RTX 5080's by a median of 6.2\% at 32 threads per warp and 9.0\%
at 8 (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_halves}). The H100's WGMMA scales with $K$ like FourShadow
at $\mathit{NRC}{=}32$ (\ref{sec:eval_nrc}) and shows the same delay for $A$ (\ref{sec:eval_delay_a}) and the same falling
halvable share (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_wg}). Table~\ref{tab:tcu-parts} gives the tensor core's
resources per part, and \ref{sec:eval_asic} its synthesized area against performance
(Figs.~\ref{fig:pareto} and~\ref{fig:iw_area_scaling}).

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{5. Attributions rest on cycle-level evidence.} [D][E] A warp-group step costs a
fixed micro-op sequence plus about one cycle per 64-byte operand line (\ref{sec:eval_threads}).
With four warps, the kernel issues 0.42--0.44 instructions per cycle and uses 1.5--1.7\% of the
shared-memory bank bandwidth; with warps grown to match an issue width of 4 or 8, the core
schedules a warp in 93--99\% of cycles, and sparsity shortens the fixed part of a
step from 72 to 53 cycles in RS mode (\ref{sec:eval_iw}). The SS delay is traced to the $A$ path
(\ref{sec:eval_delay_a}). On skinny GEMMs, tall and wide with one output side of 16 to 64 (\ref{sec:skinny_method}), the diminishing issue-width
return (\ref{sec:skinny_iw}) and the 1.42--1.54$\times$ warp-group sparsity gain (\ref{sec:skinny_sparse}) hold, as do the sparse findings of \ref{sec:large_sym}--\ref{sec:large_wg} at $256{\times}256{\times}K$
and $512{\times}512{\times}K$.

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{6. Sparse speedups are plotted raw and explained.} [D][E]
Fig.~\ref{fig:sparse_sym_sweep} prints raw speedups from total kernel cycles: the asymmetric
layouts gain 1.24--1.78$\times$ (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_sym}). A cost model fitted to measured $K$
sweeps replaces the analytic one (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_halves}). With dense and sparse kernels
staged alike, warp-group sparsity gains 1.41--1.53$\times$ (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_wg}); the
submitted ratios above 2$\times$ compared differently staged kernels.

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{7. The 16-thread optimum follows from each lane's dot product.} [B][D]
Eqs.~(\ref{eq:block_cap})--(\ref{eq:block_tc}) give 4, 8 and 8 FP16 MACs per lane at 8, 16 and
32 threads: from 16 to 32 threads only $\mathit{tcM}$ doubles (\ref{sec:eval_threads}, Answer \#1
for Q2), and neither equation has a register term. Fig.~\ref{fig:q2_nt_iw}(a) compares per-lane
FP16 throughput across thread counts at one precision. FourShadow's 32 registers per thread (24 for tensor core (\ref{sec:bg_tcu})) match an H100 SM at full occupancy (64K registers over 2,048 threads); There can only be 8 active lanes in a 32-thread warp at the 255-register maximum limit.

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{8. The roofline plots each mode's achieved point.} [B]
\ref{sec:eval_roofline} places scalar, WMMA, WGMMA and sparse execution by achieved
intensity and throughput. WGMMA reaches the highest intensity, 5.82--7.11 FLOP/byte, and an SS
step reads one $B$ tile per warpgroup, as in the RTL (\ref{sec:design_wgmma}).

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{9. The artifact will be released.} [B] The page-1 footnote commits to releasing
the RTL, simulator, kernels and figure scripts in a public repository, with its URL in the
camera-ready version. Table~\ref{tab:config} and \ref{sec:method_hw} name each swept build option
and its default, and \ref{sec:method_sw} the sweeps' kernels and shapes.

\noindent\textbf{\emph{Addressing comments for authors.}}

\vspace{1pt}
\noindent\textbf{10. We also addressed these, review by review.}
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[A]}~The limits are measured, not assumed: \ref{sec:eval_iw} traces the diminishing
issue-width return to the front end, which fetches one warp instruction per cycle.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[B]}~Table~\ref{tab:related_work_comparison} places FourShadow among prior platforms,
Accel-Sim and GPGPU-Sim included; the symmetric/asymmetric result is plotted raw
(\ref{sec:eval_sparse_sym}); ``Figure~??'', the typos and the overlapping labels of submitted
Figs.~19/20 are fixed (Figs.~\ref{fig:pareto} and~\ref{fig:iw_area_scaling}). WGMMA's 2.31
FLOP/cycle against WMMA's 23.21 (\ref{sec:eval_roofline}) counts staging: lacking DXA, our TMA
counterpart (\ref{sec:bg_smem}), warps copy $A$ and $B$ via registers into shared memory before
each warp-group MMA (\ref{sec:eval_iw}), while WMMA computes from registers. The
revision adds DXA staging, which hides the transfer of $A$ (\ref{sec:eval_sparse_wg}).
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[C]}~Section~\ref{sec:background} describes what FourShadow inherits from Vortex and
Ten-Four, and \ref{sec:design_overview} names the two components it adds.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[D]}~``Figure~??'' now points to Fig.~\ref{fig:figure_integration}, and ten evaluation
figures print their labels at 6.5\,pt or more. Sparse inputs are weights 2:4-pruned offline by magnitude; timing covers device execution only, and cycle counts do not depend on the kept values, so accuracy on pruned models does not affect the speedups.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[E]}~\ref{sec:eval_threads} states the block shape at each thread count, and
Section~\ref{sec:intro} bounds how far FourShadow's best point carries over to a commercial design.
\par\noindent\hangindent=1em\hangafter=1 \textbf{[F]}~Issue width is defined (\ref{sec:bg_issue}, \ref{sec:method_hw}).
Section~\ref{sec:design} is design only; its trade-off studies are
\ref{sec:eval_nrc}--\ref{sec:eval_desc}. The mapping figure sits in \ref{sec:bg_tcu}, which
defines $A$--$D$ and $M$, $N$, $K$ before Eq.~(\ref{eq:fedp}). \ref{sec:design_sparse} opens with 2:4
sparsity and loads the metadata into a per-warp SRAM before the sparse MMAs; the
cores-per-socket study is removed.\par
