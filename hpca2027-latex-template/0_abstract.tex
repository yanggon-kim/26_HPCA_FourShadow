\begin{abstract}
Modern GPU Tensor Cores have evolved substantially across generations, incorporating wider matrix operations, cooperative multi-warp execution, shared-memory operand delivery, and structured sparsity to sustain increasing AI throughput. However, it remains unclear how Tensor Core microarchitecture should scale across the SIMT hierarchy and which architectural parameters ultimately limit this scaling. To this end, we present FourShadow, an architectural study and exploration framework for investigating SIMT Tensor Core scaling across the threads/warp, warps/warpgroup, and cores/cluster hierarchy, built on the configurable open-source RISC-V-based Vortex GPGPU. We analyze how accumulator capacity, issue-width, operand-staging mode, dot-product length, shared-memory descriptor loading, and micro-op scheduling affect MMA throughput, utilization, and operational intensity. We further examine how 2:4 structured sparsity and 2K-wide FEDP circuitry interacts with warpgroup MMA and show that its effectiveness depends on symmetric versus asymmetric Tensor Core configurations and register file versus shared-memory staging of the pruned and compressed matrix. Evaluated on an AMD Xilinx Alveo U55C FPGA at 250 MHz Fmax, FourShadow achieves 9.6× and 2.1× end-to-end speedups over the baseline TCU for Llama3 prefill and decode, respectively.
\end{abstract}
