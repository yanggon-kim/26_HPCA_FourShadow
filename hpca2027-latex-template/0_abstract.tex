\begin{abstract}
Modern GPU Tensor Cores have evolved substantially across generations, incorporating wider matrix operations, cooperative multi-warp execution, shared-memory operand delivery, and structured sparsity to sustain increasing AI throughput. However, it remains unclear how Tensor Core microarchitecture should scale across the SIMT hierarchy and which architectural parameters ultimately limit this scaling. To this end, we present FourShadow, an architectural study and exploration framework for investigating SIMT Tensor Core scaling across the threads/warp and warps/warpgroup hierarchy, built on the configurable open-source RISC-V-based Vortex GPGPU. We analyze how accumulator capacity, issue-width, operand-staging mode, dot-product length, shared-memory descriptor loading, and micro-op scheduling affect MMA throughput, utilization, and operational intensity. We further examine 2:4 structured sparsity and show that its benefit depends on whether the thread count yields a symmetric or asymmetric Tensor Core layout for warp-level MMA, and that for warpgroup MMA the share of the per-$K$ cost that sparsity halves, and with it the attainable speedup, falls as the tile widens. Evaluated on an AMD Xilinx Alveo U55C FPGA at 250 MHz Fmax, FourShadow achieves 9.6× and 2.1× end-to-end speedups over the baseline TCU for Llama3 prefill and decode, respectively.
\end{abstract}
